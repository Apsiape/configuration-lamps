\section{Proof of Theorem~B}\label{sec:main-channel-proof}
%======================================================================

Theorem~B, in the form of Theorem~\ref{thm:main-channel}, is assembled from sequential extraction, the weighted one-round theorem, the purity
budget and the sofic-model devices. Two remarks after the proof say what it shows.

\emph{Sofic models give the closure and the services.} By Theorem~\ref{thm:main-group}(b) the chunk $E$ of $\Gamma$ that consists of $1$ and
the labels of the gadget of $R_\Phi$ has $\log\sigma_E(r)\le C_Er\log r$. Proposition~\ref{prop:sofic-upper} therefore gives finite tracial
channels within $K_G/r$ of $\Phi$ for every $r$, so $\Phi\in\cK_{873}$. This also follows from~\cite{DouglasMghirbiB}, since $\Gamma$ is amenable
(Lemma~\ref{lem:structure}) and hence hyperlinear. The exact services of part~(a) are those of Proposition~\ref{prop:sofic-upper}(c), and the
zero-purity services are those of Proposition~\ref{prop:sofic-upper}(a) with $\epsilon=1/16$. Their memory bounds follow from
$\log\sigma_E(R)\le C_ER\log R$ for $R\ge2$ and from $\log(4K_Gn/B)\le(1+\log2K_G)\log(2n/B)$, which holds because $\log(2n/B)\ge1$.
Corollary~\ref{cor:streaming-lamps} gives the services of part~(b). The bounds on $r_*$ were proved in Section~\ref{sec:lamp-channel}.

\emph{Part (a).} Let a service have error $\epsilon\le1/16$ and $P+C\le B$, where $\log n\le B$. Theorem~\ref{thm:extraction} gives a round with
bath dimension $2^Q$, $\ddia(\Psi_t,\Phi)\le\epsilon/(1-\epsilon)\le1/15$ and $\nu_t=\ell_t+\Delta S_t\le t_n$. Since $\epsilon+h_2(\epsilon)<1$,
$\epsilon\log n\le\log n$ and $1\le\log n\le B$,
\[
t_n\le\frac{16}{15}\cdot\frac{B+1+\log n}{n}\le\frac{16B}{5n}\qquad(n\ge2).
\]
Let $C_R\ge1$ be the constant of Corollary~\ref{cor:amplification}(a) for $R_\Phi$ and the transport of Section~\ref{sec:lamp-channel}, and put
$y_n=5n/(16C_RB)$, so that $C_R\nu_ty_n\le1$ and $y_n\le n$. Put $c_0=5/(16C_Ry_0)$. If $B\le c_0n$, then $y_n\ge y_0$, and the corollary gives
\[
Q\ \ge\ S(\rho_t)\ \ge\ \frac{y_n}{32(\log y_n)^{2\kappa}}\ \ge\ \frac{5}{512\,C_R}\cdot\frac{n}{B(\log n)^{2\kappa}} .
\]
For the second statement take $B=\max(1,A_0)\log n$, which lies between $\log n$ and $c_0n$ for large $n$.

\emph{Part (b).} Proposition~\ref{prop:purity-budget}, with the flat probe of rank $r_*$, gives $P+C\le B:=3Q+6$. Theorem~\ref{thm:extraction} now
gives a round with $\nu_t\le t_n\le\frac{16}{15}(3Q+7+\log n)/n$. Put $y_n=15n/(16C_R(3Q+7+\log n))\le n$. If $y_n<y_0$, then
$3Q+7+\log n>15n/(16C_Ry_0)$, so $Q\ge c'n$ for large $n$. Otherwise Corollary~\ref{cor:amplification}(a) gives $Q\ge y_n/(32(\log n)^{2\kappa})$,
that is,
\[
Q\,(3Q+7+\log n)\ \ge\ \frac{15}{512\,C_R}\cdot\frac{n}{(\log n)^{2\kappa}} .
\]
If $Q\le\log n+7$ the left side is at most $4(\log n+7)^2$, which is smaller than the right side for large $n$. So $Q>\log n+7$, the left side is
at most $4Q^2$, and $Q\ge c\sqrt n/(\log n)^\kappa$. \qed

\paragraph{Finite tracial approximations.} The same argument has a static reading. A finite tracial factorization of bath dimension $M$ is a
round with the flat bath state $\tau_M$: its entropy production vanishes, and its support leakage is at most its Choi distance to $\Phi$. So
Theorem~\ref{thm:weighted} also reads the static approximation profile of $\Phi$, and the hash models bound it from above. For channels
$\Psi,\Psi'$ on $M_d$ let $d_J(\Psi,\Psi')=\frac12\|\mathcal J(\Psi)-\mathcal J(\Psi')\|_1$ be the distance of their normalized Choi states, so
that $d_J\le\ddia\le d\,d_J$~\cite[Section~2]{DouglasMghirbiB}. For $u\ge0$ let $D_\Phi(u)$ be the least $M$ for which some channel $\Psi$ with a
finite tracial factorization of bath dimension $M$ has $d_J(\Psi,\Phi)\le u$; this is the Choi profile
of~\cite[Definition~2.1]{DouglasMghirbiB}. Let $D^\diamond_\Phi(u)$ be the same with $\ddia$ in place of $d_J$, and $D^{\mathrm{mix}}_\Phi(u)$
the same with $\Psi$ a finite convex combination of channels with finite tracial factorizations of bath dimensions at most $M$. Each is
$+\infty$ if no such $\Psi$ exists, each is nonincreasing in $u$, and $D^{\mathrm{mix}}_\Phi\le D_\Phi\le D^\diamond_\Phi\le D_\Phi(\,\cdot\,/d)$.

\begin{corollary}[Finite tracial approximations of the lamp channel]\label{cor:profile}
Let $\Phi$ be the channel of Theorem~\ref{thm:main-channel} on $M_{873}$, let $\kappa=6+\log107$, let $E$ be the chunk of $\Gamma$
consisting of $1$ and the labels of $\Phi$, and let $K_G=298+\binom{r_*}2=8683$ be its constant in Proposition~\ref{prop:sofic-upper}.
There are constants $c,C>0$ and $u_0>0$ such that for $0<u\le u_0$
\[
c\,\frac{u^{-1}}{(\log(1/u))^{2\kappa}}\le\log D^{\mathrm{mix}}_\Phi(u)\le\log D_\Phi(u)\le\log D^\diamond_\Phi(u)\le\log\sigma_E(K_G/u)\le C\,u^{-1}\log(1/u),
\]
where $2\kappa<25.49$. So the least bath dimension of a finite tracial approximation of $\Phi$ to accuracy $u$, in Choi or in diamond
distance, is $\exp(u^{-1+o(1)})$, and $D^{\mathrm{mix}}_\Phi(0)=\infty$: $\Phi$ is a limit of channels with finite tracial factorizations
but is not a finite convex combination of such channels, although it has a Stinespring dilation with a pure environment of dimension
$r_*=130$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Upper bound.} Let $0<u\le\frac12$ and $r=K_G/u\ge2$. Since $\sigma_E(r)$ is finite by Theorem~\ref{thm:main-group}(b), there is a
$(1-r^{-1})$-expansive $r^{-1}$-morphism $f:E\to\Sym(\Omega)$ with $|\Omega|=\sigma_E(r)$. Proposition~\ref{prop:sofic-upper} gives a channel
$\Phi_f=(1-\vartheta)\Phi+\vartheta\,\Xi$ with a finite tracial factorization of bath dimension $\sigma_E(r)$ and $0\le\vartheta\le K_G/r=u$, so
$\ddia(\Phi_f,\Phi)=\vartheta\,\ddia(\Xi,\Phi)\le u$ and $D^\diamond_\Phi(u)\le\sigma_E(K_G/u)$. By Theorem~\ref{thm:main-group}(b),
$\log\sigma_E(r)\le C_Er\log r$, and $\log(K_G/u)\le(1+\log K_G)\log(1/u)$ since $\log(1/u)\ge1$; so $C=C_EK_G(1+\log K_G)$ serves. The
inequalities between the three profiles hold because $d_J\le\ddia$ and a single factorization is a convex combination with one term.

\emph{A mixture is a flat round.} Let $\Psi=\sum_{i=1}^kp_i\Psi_i$ with $d_J(\Psi,\Phi)\le u$, where
$\Psi_i(X)=(\mathrm{id}\otimes\tau_{m_i})[U_i(X\otimes I)U_i^*]$ and $m_i\le M$. By Carath\'eodory's theorem we may take $k\le d^4$, since
the channels on $M_d$ lie in a real affine space of dimension less than $d^4$. On $\mathbb C^d\otimes\bigoplus_i\mathbb C^{m_i}$ put
$U=\bigoplus_iU_i$ and $\rho=\bigoplus_ip_i\tau_{m_i}$. Then the partial trace of $U(X\otimes\rho)U^*$ over $\bigoplus_i\mathbb C^{m_i}$ is
$\Psi(X)$, so $\Psi$ is a round in the sense of Section~\ref{sec:channel-setting}, with $S(\rho)=H(p)+\sum_ip_i\log m_i\le4\log d+\log M$. Its
entropy production vanishes: $T(\rho)=\bigoplus_ip_i\,\mathrm{Tr}_d\,U_i(\tau_d\otimes\tau_{m_i})U_i^*=\rho$, because
$\tau_d\otimes\tau_{m_i}$ is the normalized identity and $U_i$ is unitary. Its support leakage is
$\ell=\Tr[(I-\Pi_\Phi)(\mathcal J(\Psi)-\mathcal J(\Phi))]\le\frac12\|\mathcal J(\Psi)-\mathcal J(\Phi)\|_1\le u$, because $\mathcal J(\Phi)$
is supported in $\Pi_\Phi$ and $\Tr[PX]\le\frac12\|X\|_1$ for a projection $P$ and a traceless Hermitian $X$. So $\nu=\ell+\Delta S\le u$, and
$\ddia(\Psi,\Phi)\le d\,u\le\frac18$ when $u\le1/(8d)$: the round satisfies the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:weighted}.

\emph{Lower bound.} Section~\ref{sec:lamp-channel} verifies the hypothesis of Corollary~\ref{cor:amplification}(a) for $R_\Phi$ with the
present $\kappa$ and $K=C_1+64$. Let $C_R=2^{50}m^4dK^2\ge1$ and $y_0$ be the constants of that corollary, and put $y=1/(C_Ru)$. Then
$C_R\nu y\le1$, and $y\ge y_0$ when $u\le1/(C_Ry_0)$. For $u\le\min\{1/(8d),1/(C_Ry_0)\}$ the corollary gives, since $1<y\le1/u$,
\[
4\log d+\log M\ \ge\ S(\rho)\ \ge\ \frac{y}{32(\log y)^{2\kappa}}\ \ge\ \frac1{32C_R}\cdot\frac{u^{-1}}{(\log(1/u))^{2\kappa}} .
\]
Let $u_0$ be so small that the right side is at least $8\log d$ for $u\le u_0$. Then $\log M\ge c\,u^{-1}(\log(1/u))^{-2\kappa}$ with
$c=1/(64C_R)$, and with $c=1/(32C_R)$ for a single factorization, where $H(p)=0$. Finally $D^{\mathrm{mix}}_\Phi(0)\ge D^{\mathrm{mix}}_\Phi(u)$
for every $u>0$, and the lower bound is unbounded as $u\to0$. The pure environment is the Stinespring dilation of Remark~\ref{rem:plus-one}.
\end{proof}

By a theorem of Haagerup and Musat~\cite[Theorem~3.6]{HaagerupMusat2015}, a channel on $M_d$ lies in $\cK_d$ exactly when it factors through an
ultrapower of the hyperfinite $\mathrm{II}_1$ factor, and Musat and R{\o}rdam showed that such a channel may have no finite tracial
factorization at all~\cite{MusatRordam2020}. The Houghton channel of~\cite[Theorem~D.1]{DouglasMghirbiB} is an explicit channel of this kind,
with profile exponent between $0.069$ and $1/3$. For $\Phi$ the exponent is $1-o(1)$, nearly the most that the one-round method can certify
(Remark~\ref{rem:sqrt-limit}), while channels in the relative interior of $\cK_d$ have profiles at most linear in
$1/u$~\cite[Section~10]{DouglasMghirbiB}.

\begin{remark}[Memory between two readings of the profile]\label{rem:sandwich}
Take a gadget channel of a sofic group that satisfies the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:weighted}. The least memory of its services with error
$1/16$ and purity $O(\log n)$ lies between two readings of the group at scale $n$. The lower reading is the entropy forced by as many cheaply
commuting copies of $S_3$ as Corollary~\ref{cor:amplification} fits at relator defect about $\sqrt{\log n/n}$. The upper reading is
$\log\sigma_E(Cn)+O(\log n)$, by Proposition~\ref{prop:sofic-upper}; it is attained already at zero purity, with exchange $(n-1)Q$. For the
configuration lamps both readings are $n^{1+o(1)}$. The upper reading is the sofic profile, not the F{\o}lner function. The latter is doubly
exponential for $\Gamma$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:folner-lower}), so models built from F{\o}lner sets~\cite[Proposition~3.14]{Cornulier2013} give
memory exponential in $n$, while the hash models behind Theorem~\ref{thm:main-group}(b) give $O(n\log n)$. The grid host gives a second pair
of readings, conditionally on the sketches of Appendix~\ref{sec:grid} (Remark~\ref{rem:grid-channel}).
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[The rank rate is the diagonal of the trade-off]\label{rem:sqrt-limit}
At the rank rate a device with error at most $1/16$ has purity less than $3Q+6$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:purity-budget}).
Theorem~\ref{thm:main-channel}(a) with $B$ of order $Q$ therefore forces $Q\gtrsim\sqrt n$, and the streaming services attain this with purity
of the same order. In general, for every channel with a flat probe, padding a rank-rate service with error at most $1/16$ by $2Q+6$ maximally mixed
qubits turns it into a service whose purity is at most its memory. The least memory at purity at most memory is therefore at most three times
the least memory at the rank rate, plus six. For $\Phi$ both are $n^{1/2+o(1)}$. The one-round method cannot give more than $\sqrt n$ at the
rank rate, since condition~\eqref{eq:weighted-condition} certifies at most about $1/\nu$ copies; Theorem~\ref{thm:streaming} shows that for
$\Phi$ nothing more is true.
\end{remark}
