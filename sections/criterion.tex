\section{Counting approximately commuting copies of \texorpdfstring{$S_3$}{S3}}\label{sec:criterion}
%======================================================================

Every lower bound in this paper comes from one principle, proved in~\cite{DouglasMghirbiA}. If finitely many relations force many displaced
copies of $S_3$ to commute cheaply, every model of a fixed chunk must be large, because approximately commuting copies of $S_3$ carry
entropy. We fix the conventions used in both halves of the paper, state the principle in the form used here, with $a,b$ in place of the
letters $u,v$ of~\cite{DouglasMghirbiA}, and note the ceiling $\exp(O(r))$ that it can never pass. The scheme, many cheaply transported
copies of a finite group, relations whose cost is controlled by area, and a dimension bound, follows Slofstra~\cite[Sections~2--3]{Slofstra2018}.
The pairwise criterion needs only approximate commutation of displaced copies of $S_3$; Theorem~\ref{thm:sweep} strengthens its permutation form using whole suffixes.

\paragraph{Conventions.} Functions are composed from right to left, groups act on the left, $[x,y]=xyx^{-1}y^{-1}$, and $\log$ is to base
two. For a finite set $S$ and a finite set $R$ of words in $S^{\pm1}$, $\Area_R(w)$ is the least number of conjugates of words of $R^{\pm1}$
whose product is $w$ in the free group on $S$, and $\infty$ if there is none. A \emph{chunk} of a group is a finite subset $E\ni1$ with the
partial multiplication $xy=z$ whenever $x,y,z\in E$ and $xy=z$ in the group. Its \emph{sofic profile} $\sigma_E(r)$ is the least $n$ for which
some $f:E\to\Sym_n$ with $f(1)=\mathrm{id}$ multiplies and separates correctly up to $1/r$: $d(f(x)f(y),f(xy))\le1/r$ whenever $xy$ is defined in
$E$, and $d(f(x),f(y))\ge1-1/r$ for distinct $x,y\in E$~\cite{Cornulier2013}. Here $d$ is the normalized Hamming distance, the fraction of points
on which two permutations differ. The normalized Hilbert--Schmidt norm of a $D\times D$ matrix is $\|X\|_2=(\Tr(X^*X)/D)^{1/2}$.

The criterion is Theorem~8.1 of~\cite{DouglasMghirbiA}, and Appendix~\ref{app:imported} reproduces its proof.

\begin{theorem}[Counting criterion]\label{thm:criterion}
Let $G$ be a group, $S\subset G$ a finite set containing $a,b$, and $R$ a finite set of words in $S^{\pm1}$, each representing $1$ in $G$, with
$a^2,b^2,(ab)^3\in R$ and $ab\ne1$ in $G$. Let $l$ be the largest length of a word in $R$, and let $E$ be the chunk consisting of $1$, $S^{\pm1}$,
the prefixes of the words of $R$ and of $ab$, and the inverses of these elements. Let $w_1,\dots,w_N$ be words in $S^{\pm1}$ and $\Lambda\ge1$ such
that
\[
\Area_R\bigl([w_ixw_i^{-1},w_jyw_j^{-1}]\bigr)\le\Lambda\qquad(i\ne j,\ x,y\in\{a,b\}).
\]
Then $\log\sigma_E(r)\ge N/16$ for every $r\ge10^8N\Lambda(2l-1)$. Moreover, for $0<\Delta\le1$ and
$K_\Delta=\sqrt{80000\,(1+\log(1/\Delta))}/\Delta$, every assignment $\phi$ of unitaries in $U(D)$ to $S$ under which every word of $R$ lies
within $e$ of $I$ in the normalized Hilbert--Schmidt norm and $\|\phi(ab)-I\|_2\ge\Delta$ satisfies $\log D\ge N\Delta^2/12$ whenever
$K_\Delta\sqrt N\,\Lambda e\le1$.
\end{theorem}

The proof in~\cite{DouglasMghirbiA} has four steps, and only the last is specific to $S_3$. Conjugating the images of $a$ and $b$ by the image
of $w_i$ gives $N$ copies of $S_3$, one for each word. Each copy satisfies the relations of $S_3$ up to the defect of a relator, because those
relations are conjugates of $a^2$, $b^2$ and $(ab)^3$, so it can be rounded to an exact representation of $S_3$. Its three-cycle is a conjugate
of the image of $ab$, so it is displaced from the identity as much as that image is. Two copies commute up to $\Lambda$ times the relator defect,
because each of their commutators is a product of at most $\Lambda$ conjugates of relators. Finally, exact copies of $S_3$ that nearly commute
and have displaced three-cycles cannot share a small space: they force a fixed amount of entropy per copy, at least $\Delta^2/12$ bits for
unitaries and $1/16$ bit for permutations~\cite[Section~4]{DouglasMghirbiA}. The chunk $E$ does not depend on $N$ or on the words $w_i$; only
the areas matter.

The theorem has a ceiling. Its hypotheses force $N\le r/10^8$ and $N\le(K_\Delta e)^{-2}$, so it never gives $\log\sigma_E(r)$ beyond order $r$,
nor $\log D$ beyond order $e^{-2}$. Below the ceiling, everything depends on how cheaply the group carries copies of $S_3$ to many places.
Transport by the powers of one element, with area of order $k^p$ at distance $k$, gives exponent $1/(p+1)$; this is Corollary~8.2
of~\cite{DouglasMghirbiA}.

\begin{corollary}[Transport by the powers of one element]\label{cor:cyclic}
Let $G$, $S$, $R$, $l$ and $E$ be as in Theorem~\ref{thm:criterion}, let $t\in S$, and suppose that
$\Area_R([x,t^kyt^{-k}])\le Ck^p$ for $x,y\in\{a,b\}$ and $k\ge1$, with $C\ge1$ and $p\ge0$. Then
\[
\log\sigma_E(r)\ \ge\ \frac1{16}\Big\lfloor\Big(\frac r{10^8C(2l-1)}\Big)^{1/(p+1)}\Big\rfloor\qquad(r>1),
\]
and every unitary assignment as in Theorem~\ref{thm:criterion} has $\log D\ge\frac{\Delta^2}{12}\bigl((CK_\Delta e)^{-2/(2p+1)}-1\bigr)$.
\end{corollary}

In Houghton's group $H_3$ the copies of $S_3$ along a ray have commutators of at least quadratic area~\cite[Proposition~3.4]{DouglasMghirbiA},
so the pairwise estimate of Corollary~\ref{cor:cyclic} never passes exponent $1/3$ along that ray. Whole suffix sweeps can improve this conclusion, as Theorem~\ref{thm:sweep} shows.

One more fact of Cornulier's is used twice, in Sections~\ref{sec:more} and~\ref{sec:ladder}. The profile of a chunk is bounded exactly when the
chunk embeds in a finite group, and otherwise it is at least $r$~\cite[Fact~3.5]{Cornulier2013}, indeed at least
$2r$~\cite[Lemma~2.1]{DouglasMghirbiA}. A chunk with unbounded profile therefore certifies that its group is not locally embeddable into finite
groups, not LEF~\cite[Fact~3.8]{Cornulier2013}.

\subsection{Whole suffix sweeps}

Pairwise commutator estimates can overcount the cost of moving one copy past an ordered suffix. The following form charges one filling
for the whole suffix. It uses permutation rounding in Hamming distance before passing to the entropy estimate.

\begin{theorem}[Sweep counting criterion]\label{thm:sweep}
Use the group, relators, chunk and transport words of Theorem~\ref{thm:criterion}, and suppose $N\ge1$.
For each of the six fixed normal forms
\[
\mathcal N=\{1,a,b,ab,ba,aba\},\qquad W_i(g)=w_i g w_i^{-1},
\]
assume that, for every $i$, every $h\in\mathcal N$ and every choice $g_j\in\mathcal N$ for $j>i$,
\[
\Area_R\left([W_i(h),W_{i+1}(g_{i+1})\cdots W_N(g_N)]\right)\le\Lambda_N.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sweep-criterion}
 (\Lambda_N+40N)(2l-1)\le r/20000
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \log\sigma_E(r)\ge N/16.
\end{equation}
If only the sweeps with $h=a,b$ are bounded by $\Lambda_N$, the same conclusion holds with $r/60000$ in the
hypothesis in place of $r/20000$. In both versions every later factor ranges over all six normal forms.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Relator errors.} Given a chunk model $f:E\to\Sym_n$, evaluate generator words by $\phi(s)=f(s)$ and
$\phi(s^{-1})=f(s)^{-1}$. Merging successive prefixes of a relator of length $m$ costs at most $(m-1)/r$;
replacing inverse letters costs at most $m/r$, since $s,s^{-1},1\in E$. Thus each relator has Hamming defect at most
$\epsilon=(2l-1)/r$. Bi-invariance implies that a word of area $A$ has defect at most $A\epsilon$.
The prefix of $ab$ and expansiveness give $d(\phi(ab),1)\ge1-2/r$. All transported pairs have the same square and braid
errors and three-cycle displacement: conjugation is an exact Hamming isometry, regardless of the transport word's length.

\emph{Native rounding.} Let $A,B$ be any transported pair. Replace cycles of length at least three in each permutation by fixed points,
obtaining involutions $P,Q$ with $d(P,A),d(Q,B)\le\epsilon$. Put $R=PQ$. Then
$d(R^3,1)\le7\epsilon$. The identity $QRQ=R^{-1}$ implies that $\mathrm{Fix}(R^3)$ is invariant under $Q,R$, and hence under $P=RQ$.
Keep $P,Q$ on this set and make them both trivial on its invariant complement. They now give an exact permutation representation
$u_i:S_3\to\Sym_n$. Every normal form has length at most three: its first replacement costs at most $3\epsilon$, and deleting bad
orbits costs at most $7\epsilon$. Consequently
\[
 d(u_i(g),\phi(W_i(g)))\le10\epsilon\qquad(g\in\mathcal N).
\]
This construction allows trivial and sign orbits. In particular, the three-cycle loses at most $10\epsilon$ of its Hamming displacement.

\emph{One ordered error.} Put $U_{>i}=u_{i+1}(g_{i+1})\cdots u_N(g_N)$ and let $V_{>i}$ be its raw-word counterpart.
Replacing both occurrences of the first factor and of the suffix in the commutator gives
\[
 d([u_i(h),U_{>i}],1)
 \le\bigl(\Lambda_N+20(N-i+1)\bigr)\epsilon
 \le(\Lambda_N+40N)\epsilon.
\]
Let $F(g)=u_1(g_1)\cdots u_N(g_N)$, for uniform $g\in S_3^N$. Exact same-copy multiplication shows that the error
$F(gh)-F(g)u_i(h)$ is exactly the error in commuting $u_i(h)$ past its suffix, up to unitary factors.
For permutation matrices $\|P-Q\|_2^2=2d(P,Q)$; hence
\[
 T^2:=\max_{i,h}\mathbb E_g\|F(gh)-F(g)u_i(h)\|_2^2
 \le2(\Lambda_N+40N)\epsilon.
\]
Here $h$ lies in factor $i$. There is no same-copy multiplication defect after rounding.

\emph{Entropy from this error.} We give the sector argument to specify exactly what is used. Suppose
$\|u_i(c)-I\|_2\ge\Delta$ for a three-cycle $c$, $T\le\Delta$ and $T^2\le5/6$.
On $\ell^2(S_3^N)\otimes\mathbb C^n$ use the regular action $\mathsf R$ and the isometry
\[
 Wv=|S_3^N|^{-1/2}\sum_g|g\rangle\otimes F(g)v,
 \qquad \Pi=WW^*,\quad\rho=\Pi/n.
\]
The ordered-error identity gives $\|\mathsf R(h_i)W-Wu_i(h)\|_{HS}\le\sqrt n\,T$.
Thus, for $Z_i=2I-\mathsf R(c_i)-\mathsf R(c_i)^*$,
$\Tr(\rho Z_i)\ge(\Delta-T)^2$. Let $\mathcal E_i$ average conjugation by the six regular operators in factor $i$.
Its leakage outside $\Pi$ is at most $T^2$. Pinching into $\Pi$ and $I-\Pi$ gives ranks at most $n$ and $5n$, because one of the
six states being averaged is $\rho$. Therefore
$S(\mathcal E_i\rho)\le\log n+h_2(T^2)+T^2\log5$.
The $\mathcal E_i$ commute. Monotonicity of relative entropy, together with
$S(\mathcal E\sigma)-S(\sigma)=D(\sigma\|\mathcal E\sigma)$, bounds each successive entropy increment by its increment on $\rho$.
For $\bar\rho=\mathcal E_N\cdots\mathcal E_1\rho$ this yields
\[
 S(\bar\rho)\le\log n+N\{h_2(T^2)+T^2\log5\}.
\]
On each irreducible sector of $S_3^N$, $Z_i$ is $3I$ if factor $i$ is standard and zero otherwise.
If a sector has $k$ standard factors, its dimension is $2^k$. Since $\bar\rho$ commutes with the regular action, its entropy is
at least the mean of $k$. The twirls preserve the expectation of each central $Z_i$, so
$N(\Delta-T)^2\le3S(\bar\rho)$. Combining gives
\[
 \log n\ge N\{(\Delta-T)^2/3-h_2(T^2)-T^2\log5\}.
\]
No pairwise estimate entered this argument.

\emph{Constants.} The stated hypothesis gives $T\le1/100$ and
$\epsilon\le1/(800000N)$. The preceding displacement estimate gives $\Delta\ge49/100$ (a deliberately conservative
Hilbert--Schmidt lower bound). Using $\log_2(10000)<14$ and $1/\ln2<3/2$, one has
\[
 h_2(1/10000)\le31/20000,\qquad
 (1/10000)\log5\le6/20000.
\]
Thus the entropy per copy is at least
$(48/100)^2/3-37/20000=1499/20000>1/16$.
For elementary-letter sweeps, split $h$ into at most three letters and use the free commutator product identity.
The full sweep area is at most $3\Lambda_N$, and
$3\Lambda_N+40N\le3(\Lambda_N+40N)$, proving the $1/60000$ version.
\end{proof}

The $40N$ term retains the counting method's linear scale in $r$ even if sweep areas are very small. This is a restriction
on lower bounds produced by the criterion, not an upper bound on any group's actual profile.
The transported finite-group and area-to-defect strategy follows Slofstra and Slofstra--Vidick~\cite{Slofstra2018,SlofstraVidick2018};
metric approximation growth with growing balls, as studied by Arzhantseva--Cherix and Bradford~\cite{ArzhantsevaCherix2024,Bradford2021},
is distinct from the fixed-chunk accuracy profile used here.
