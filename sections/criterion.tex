%======================================================================
\section{Counting approximately commuting copies of \texorpdfstring{$S_3$}{S3}}\label{sec:criterion}
%======================================================================

All lower bounds in this paper come from one principle, proved in~\cite{DouglasMghirbiA}: if finitely many relations force many displaced
copies of $S_3$ to commute, every model of a fixed finite piece must be large. We recall it in the form we use, with $a,b$ in place of the letters $u,v$ of~\cite{DouglasMghirbiA}. The scheme, many cheaply transported
copies of a finite group, relations whose cost is controlled by area, and a dimension bound, follows Slofstra~\cite[Sections~2--3]{Slofstra2018}; the
criterion needs only pairwise approximate commutation of displaced copies of $S_3$.

For a finite set $S$ and a finite set $R$ of words in $S^{\pm1}$, $\Area_R(w)$ denotes the least number of conjugates of words of $R^{\pm1}$
whose product is $w$ in the free group on $S$, and $\infty$ if there is none. A \emph{chunk} of a group is a finite subset $E\ni1$ with the
partial multiplication $xy=z$ whenever $x,y,z\in E$ and $xy=z$ in the group. Its sofic profile $\sigma_E(r)$ is the least $n$ for which some
$f:E\to\Sym_n$ with $f(1)=\mathrm{id}$ has $d(f(x)f(y),f(xy))\le1/r$ whenever $xy$ is defined in $E$ and $d(f(x),f(y))\ge1-1/r$ for distinct
$x,y\in E$, where $d$ is the normalized Hamming distance~\cite{Cornulier2013}. Logarithms are to base two.

\begin{theorem}[{\cite[Theorem~8.1]{DouglasMghirbiA}}]\label{thm:criterion}
Let $G$ be a group, $S\subset G$ a finite set containing $a,b$, and $R$ a finite set of words in $S^{\pm1}$, each representing $1$ in $G$, with
$a^2,b^2,(ab)^3\in R$ and $ab\ne1$ in $G$. Let $l$ be the largest length of a word in $R$, and let $E$ be the chunk consisting of $1$, $S^{\pm1}$,
the prefixes of the words of $R$ and of $ab$, and the inverses of these elements. Let $w_1,\dots,w_N$ be words in $S^{\pm1}$ and $\Lambda\ge1$ such
that
\[
\Area_R\bigl([w_ixw_i^{-1},w_jyw_j^{-1}]\bigr)\le\Lambda\qquad(i\ne j,\ x,y\in\{a,b\}).
\]
Then $\log\sigma_E(r)\ge N/16$ for every $r\ge10^8N\Lambda(2l-1)$. Moreover, for $0<\Delta\le1$ and
$K_\Delta=\sqrt{80000\,(1+\log(1/\Delta))}/\Delta$, every assignment $\phi$ of unitaries in $U(D)$ to $S$ under which every word of $R$ lies
within $e$ of $I$ in the normalized Hilbert--Schmidt norm and $\|\phi(ab)-I\|_2\ge\Delta$ satisfies $\log D\ge N\Delta^2/12$ whenever
$K_\Delta\sqrt N\,\Lambda e\le1$.
\end{theorem}

The mechanism is short to describe. Conjugating one approximate copy of $S_3$ by the words $w_i$ produces $N$ copies whose three-cycles are
displaced from the identity and which commute up to $\Lambda$ times the relator error. Each copy is rounded to an exact representation of $S_3$,
and a state on the model space made invariant under all of them must carry about one bit of entropy per copy. The chunk $E$ does not depend on
$N$ or on the words $w_i$; only the areas matter.

Two consequences frame everything below. First, the theorem never gives more than $\log\sigma_E(r)$ of order $r$, nor $\log D$ of order
$e^{-2}$: its hypotheses force $N\le r/10^8$ and $N\le(K_\Delta e)^{-2}$. Second, the result depends on how cheaply the group can carry
copies of $S_3$ to many places. When the copies are transported by the powers of one element, with area of order $k^p$ at distance $k$, the
theorem gives exponent $1/(p+1)$:

\begin{corollary}[{\cite[Corollary~8.2]{DouglasMghirbiA}}]\label{cor:cyclic}
Let $G$, $S$, $R$, $l$ and $E$ be as in Theorem~\ref{thm:criterion}, let $t\in S$, and suppose that
$\Area_R([x,t^kyt^{-k}])\le Ck^p$ for $x,y\in\{a,b\}$ and $k\ge1$, with $C\ge1$ and $p\ge0$. Then
\[
\log\sigma_E(r)\ \ge\ \frac1{16}\Big\lfloor\Big(\frac r{10^8C(2l-1)}\Big)^{1/(p+1)}\Big\rfloor\qquad(r>1),
\]
and every unitary assignment as in Theorem~\ref{thm:criterion} has $\log D\ge\frac{\Delta^2}{12}\bigl((CK_\Delta e)^{-2/(2p+1)}-1\bigr)$.
\end{corollary}

For Houghton's group $H_3$ and its copies of $S_3$ along a ray, the area grows at least quadratically~\cite[Proposition~3.4]{DouglasMghirbiA}, so
transport along a ray can never pass the exponent $1/3$. To do better, a group must reach many sites cheaply. Finally, by Cornulier's
dichotomy~\cite[Fact~3.5]{Cornulier2013} the profile of a chunk is bounded exactly when the chunk embeds in a finite group, and otherwise it is at
least $r$; this lower bound is sharpened to $2r$ in~\cite[Lemma~2.1]{DouglasMghirbiA}. So a chunk with unbounded profile certifies that its
group is not locally embeddable into finite groups (LEF)~\cite[Fact~3.8]{Cornulier2013}.
